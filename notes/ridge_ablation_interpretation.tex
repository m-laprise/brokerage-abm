At the baseline, every restriction reduces the broker's ranking advantage.
Size matching lowers the broker-minus-principal difference in holdout rank
correlation to \rav{size_matchedBaseline_rank_gap}, a paired change of
\rav{size_matchedBaselineDelta_rank_gap} from base Ridge
([\rav{size_matchedBaselineDeltaLower_rank_gap},
\rav{size_matchedBaselineDeltaUpper_rank_gap}]). The single-principal and
additive differences are \rav{single_principalBaseline_rank_gap} and
\rav{additiveBaseline_rank_gap}; their paired changes are
\rav{single_principalBaselineDelta_rank_gap}
([\rav{single_principalBaselineDeltaLower_rank_gap},
\rav{single_principalBaselineDeltaUpper_rank_gap}]) and
\rav{additiveBaselineDelta_rank_gap}
([\rav{additiveBaselineDeltaLower_rank_gap},
\rav{additiveBaselineDeltaUpper_rank_gap}]). All intervals are
\rav{mcLevelPercent}\% Monte Carlo intervals.

Size matching reduces the ranking difference in
\rav{size_matchedLessN_rank_gap}/\rav{nConditions} effective realizations. The
median change is \rav{size_matchedDeltaMedian_rank_gap}, and the difference
remains positive in
\rav{size_matchedPositiveN_rank_gap}/\rav{nConditions} realizations. This is not
only a change in broker ranking. The median broker holdout rank correlation
changes by \rav{size_matchedDeltaMedian_broker_holdout_rank}, while the median
principal correlation changes by
\rav{size_matchedDeltaMedian_agent_holdout_rank}. Principal ranking also
exceeds base Ridge in \rav{size_matchedGreaterN_agent_holdout_rank},
\rav{single_principalGreaterN_agent_holdout_rank}, and
\rav{additiveGreaterN_agent_holdout_rank} realizations under size matching,
single-principal history, and additive Ridge, respectively. Altered broker
rankings may expose agents to a broader range of partner types, but the saved
diagnostics do not measure that range. The experiment therefore does not test
this possible exploration mechanism directly.

The pair-versus-additive contrast retains both endpoint types but removes the
bilinear pair features. As Figure~\ref{fig:ridge-ablation-grid} shows, this
restriction matters most when complementarity contributes strongly to match
quality. The median additive-Ridge ranking difference rises from
\rav{additiveRho0_rank_gap} at $\rho=\rav{rhoInteriorMin}$ to
\rav{additiveRho0p85_rank_gap} at $\rho=\rav{rhoInteriorMax}$ and
\rav{additiveRho1_rank_gap} at the pure-quality boundary
$\rho=\rav{rhoBoundary}$. At that boundary, the additive representation matches
the production function and exceeds the base Ridge difference of
\rav{pairRho1_rank_gap}. This could reflect the finite-sample cost of unnecessary
bilinear regressors, but the system-level contrast does not isolate that
mechanism.

Single-principal and additive Ridge both produce additive pair scores, but only
additive Ridge retains both endpoint types in each fitting row. At the baseline,
the single-principal-minus-additive ranking difference is
\rav{singleMinusAdditiveBaseline_rank_gap}
([\rav{singleMinusAdditiveBaselineLower_rank_gap},
\rav{singleMinusAdditiveBaselineUpper_rank_gap}]). Across effective
realizations, the median difference is
\rav{singleMinusAdditiveMedian_rank_gap}, and single-principal Ridge exceeds
additive Ridge in only
\rav{singleMinusAdditiveGreaterN_rank_gap}/\rav{nConditions} realizations.
Retaining both endpoint types therefore produces a modest system-level benefit
among these two additive-score variants. Neither variant can represent the
bilinear interaction.

The brokered-minus-self output gap remains positive in all
\rav{nConditions} realizations under each restriction, even when the broker's
ranking difference is negative. This channel comparison is descriptive because
channel choice, candidate sets, and offer acceptance are endogenous. More
generally, these ablations change prediction, matching, learning histories,
outsourcing, and network position together. They estimate the consequences of
each restriction for the resulting system, not additive shares of a fixed-data
prediction advantage.
