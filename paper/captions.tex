\begin{ptitle}{position}
Broker centrality and access fraction
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{position}
Left: broker betweenness centrality and access fraction over time at the baseline, as \pv{rollWin}-observation rolling ensemble means over seeds, with pointwise 95\% Monte Carlo intervals; centrality is measured every \pv{betwInterval} periods. Right: all \pv{rhoEtaConditionN} combinations in the $\rho\times\eta$ grid, with other parameters held at baseline, including $\delta=0.5$, each shown as a late-window mean over that regime's seeds. Color identifies turnover $\eta$, marker shape identifies $\rho$, and whiskers are 95\% Monte Carlo intervals on both axes. Within each $\eta$, lines connect all $\rho$ values below 1. The pure-quality boundary $\rho=1$, where complementarity is absent, is shown unconnected. Time-series panels begin at $t=\pv{axisStart}$ to omit the initialization transient.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{assessment_access}
Brokerage with access, assessment, or both
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{assessment_access}
Broker models are defined in Section~\ref{sec:assessment-access}. $\rho=\pv{aaRho}$ and $\delta=\pv{aaDelta}$ throughout. Panel A shows differences from full service in net output to principals per requested position at $\eta\in\{\pv{aaEtas}\}$. Net output deducts search costs and broker fees; unfilled positions remain in the denominator. Panels B--D use baseline turnover ($\eta=\pv{aaEta}$). Panel B shows mean principal degree at every period from $t=\pv{aaDegreeStart}$ to $\pv{aaHorizon}$. Degree includes each principal's link to the broker when present. Panel C shows late-mean differences in broker betweenness centrality from full service. Panel D shows mean broker betweenness centrality, measured at $t=\pv{aaCentralityFirst},\pv{aaCentralitySecond},\ldots,\pv{aaHorizon}$. Panels B and D show levels for all three broker models; lines join recorded values without smoothing. Panels A and C show assessment-only and access-only minus full service, using recorded outcomes averaged over $t=\pv{aaLateStart}$--$\pv{aaHorizon}$ for each seed. Differences are calculated within each seed before averaging across seeds. Negative values indicate outcomes below full service. Bands and bars show two-sided, pointwise \pv{aaIntervalPercent}\% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals. Each simulation uses $N=\pv{aaN}$ and $T=\pv{aaHorizon}$. Estimates use \pv{aaBaselineSeeds} seeds per baseline model and \pv{aaOtherSeeds} at other turnover rates. Panel A displays zero turnover in a separate shaded strip; positive rates use a linear turnover scale. In Panel A, series are offset horizontally for visibility. Lines connect positive-turnover points as visual guides; zero-turnover points are unconnected. The shaded band on the positive-turnover axis marks baseline turnover. All other parameters remain at baseline.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{information}
Sources of the broker's ranking advantage
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{information}
Panel A shows each Ridge restriction's change in the broker-minus-principal difference in rank correlation at the baseline, calculated as the restriction minus the unrestricted Ridge specification. Negative values indicate that the restriction reduces the broker's ranking advantage. Points are mean differences calculated within matched runs using the same random seeds; whiskers are paired 95\% Monte Carlo intervals over \pv{nBaselineSeeds} seeds. Panel B shows the broker-minus-principal difference in rank correlation under the unrestricted Ridge specification and each restriction across the \pv{ablationConditionN} regimes in the $\rho\times\delta$ design, including the baseline. Thin lines connect estimates for the same regime across Ridge variants, small points are regime means, diamonds are medians, and the horizontal line marks zero.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{grid}
Outcomes across the matching problem ($\rho\times\delta$)
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{grid}
Six outcomes across the $\rho\times\delta$ grid; one line per difficulty level $\delta$, each spanning $\rho=0$ through $0.85$. The pure-quality boundary $\rho=1$, where $\delta$ has no effect, is one separate regime shown once and unconnected. The figure therefore displays all \pv{rhoDeltaEffectiveN} regimes in the design. Each marker is a late-window mean over that regime's seeds; whiskers are 95\% Monte Carlo intervals. The reported difference in rank correlation is broker minus principal; the output gap is mean realized match output through the broker minus mean realized match output through self-search.
\end{pcaption}

Difference in rank correlation (broker minus principal) and output gap (mean realized match output through the broker minus mean realized match output through self-search) against broker betweenness centrality and access fraction; one dot per regime, each a late-window mean over its seeds. \end{pcaption}
