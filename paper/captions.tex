\begin{ptitle}{position}
Broker centrality and access fraction
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{position}
Left: broker betweenness centrality and access fraction over time at the
baseline, as \pv{rollWin}-observation rolling ensemble means over
seeds, with pointwise 95\% Monte Carlo intervals; centrality is measured every
\pv{betwInterval} periods. Right: all 20 combinations in the $\rho\times\eta$
grid, with other parameters held at baseline, including $\delta=0.5$, each shown
as a late-window mean over that regime's seeds. Color identifies turnover $\eta$,
marker shape identifies $\rho$, and whiskers are 95\% Monte Carlo intervals on
both axes.
Within each $\eta$, lines connect $\rho=0,0.5,0.85$. The pure-quality boundary
$\rho=1$, where complementarity is absent, is shown unconnected. Time-series
panels begin at $t=\pv{axisStart}$ to omit the
initialization transient.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{information}
Sources of the broker's ranking advantage
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{information}
Panel A shows each Ridge restriction's change in the broker-minus-principal
difference in holdout rank correlation at the baseline, calculated as the
restriction minus the base Ridge specification. Negative values indicate that
the restriction reduces the broker's ranking advantage. Points are mean
common-seed differences; whiskers are paired 95\% Monte Carlo intervals over 50
seeds. Panel B shows the broker-minus-principal difference in holdout rank
correlation under the base Ridge specification
and each restriction across the \pv{ablationConditionN} regimes in the
$\rho\times\delta$ design, including the baseline. Thin lines connect estimates
for the same regime across Ridge variants, small points are regime means, diamonds
are medians, and the horizontal line marks zero.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{grid}
Outcomes across the matching problem ($\rho\times\delta$)
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{grid}
Six outcomes across the $\rho\times\delta$ grid; one line per difficulty level
$\delta$, each spanning $\rho=0$ through $0.85$. The pure-quality boundary
$\rho=1$, where $\delta$ has no effect, is one separate regime shown once and
unconnected. The figure therefore displays all 26 regimes in the design. Each
marker is a late-window mean over that regime's seeds; whiskers are 95\% Monte
Carlo intervals.
The reported difference in holdout rank correlation is broker minus principal;
the output gap is broker
channel minus self-search.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{advantage}
Ranking and output differences by centrality and access
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{advantage}
Difference in holdout rank correlation (broker minus principal) and output gap
(broker channel minus self-search) against broker betweenness centrality and
access fraction; one dot for each of the \pv{nRegimes} effective regimes, each a
late-window mean over its seeds. Color identifies matching composition $\rho$.
\end{pcaption}

\begin{ptitle}{dynamics}
Baseline dynamics
\end{ptitle}
\begin{pcaption}{dynamics}
Lines are \pv{rollWin}-observation rolling ensemble means over seeds at the
baseline regime; shaded ribbons are pointwise 95\% Monte Carlo intervals. Some
intervals are narrower than the plotted lines and therefore are not separately
visible. Broker betweenness centrality is measured every \pv{betwInterval}
periods; the other outcomes are measured every period. Axes start at
$t=\pv{axisStart}$ to omit the initialization transient.
\end{pcaption}
